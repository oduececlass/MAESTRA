The gate presses the ground.
Watch the water find it.

Energy bands are terrain. The Fermi level is a water table that never moves. Everything the gate does — accumulate, deplete, invert — is the same single act: pressing the ground near the surface up or down until the carriers rearrange themselves around a fixed waterline.

Reading the picture

The upper surface is the conduction-band floor — the ground electrons walk on. The lower surface is the valence-band ceiling — the roof holes cling to. Between them is bedrock: the band gap, where nothing can sit. The dashed horizontal line is the Fermi level, and it is the one thing in this picture that never moves.

Water depth is plotted on a log scale, so a puddle that looks twice as deep is orders of magnitude more carriers. That is the whole trick of a MOSFET: a small push on the ground buys an enormous change in how much water is standing on it.
