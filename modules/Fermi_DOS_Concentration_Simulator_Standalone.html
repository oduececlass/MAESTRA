Complete semiconductor statistics teaching simulator: probability concept, Fermi energy level, density of states, carrier concentration, donors/acceptors, Boltzmann approximation, freeze-out, degeneracy, temperature excitation, and worked calculations.

Concept map

The central idea is that carrier concentration is determined by both the number of available states and the probability that each state is occupied.

\[\text{occupied states}=g(E)f_F(E)\]
\[n_0=\int_{E_c}^{\infty}g_c(E)f_F(E)\,dE,\quad p_0=\int_{-\infty}^{E_v}g_v(E)[1-f_F(E)]\,dE\]

What is the Fermi energy?

The Fermi energy level \(E_F\) is the energy reference that controls occupation probability. At any temperature, a state at \(E=E_F\) has a 50% probability of being occupied.

\[f_F(E_F)=\frac{1}{2}\]

In n-type material, \(E_F\) moves toward \(E_c\). In p-type material, \(E_F\) moves toward \(E_v\).

Key visual rule

Increasing temperature does not create new states. It changes occupation probability: some electrons are excited upward, leaving empty lower-energy states behind.

Probability and quantum states

A quantum state is an available allowed energy state. Electron occupancy is probabilistic.

\[0\le f_F(E)\le1\]

Because of the Pauli exclusion principle, a quantum state cannot accept unlimited electrons. This is why semiconductor statistics is different from ordinary classical particles.

Occupied and empty probabilities

\[P(\text{occupied})=f_F(E)\]
\[P(\text{empty})=1-f_F(E)\]

Hole concentration in the valence band uses the empty-state probability \(1-f_F(E)\).

At the Fermi energy

\[E=E_F\Rightarrow f_F(E)=\frac{1}{1+e^0}=\frac12\]

Position of the Fermi energy level

The Fermi level moves with doping because the majority-carrier concentration changes exponentially with \(E_F\).

\[E_c-E_F=k_BT\ln\left(\frac{N_c}{n_0}\right)\]
\[E_F-E_v=k_BT\ln\left(\frac{N_v}{p_0}\right)\]

Compensated semiconductor

For compensation, replace donor or acceptor concentration by the net effective concentration: \(N_d-N_a\) for n-type and \(N_a-N_d\) for p-type.

Statistics of donors and acceptors

Donor and acceptor states have their own occupation probabilities. At low temperature, carriers can freeze out in impurity levels; at room temperature many shallow impurities are nearly fully ionized.

\[n_d=\frac{N_d}{1+\frac12\exp\left(\frac{E_d-E_F}{k_BT}\right)}\]
\[p_a=\frac{N_a}{1+\frac14\exp\left(\frac{E_F-E_a}{k_BT}\right)}\]

Boltzmann approximation and freeze-out

The Boltzmann approximation is valid when the state energy is several \(k_BT\) above the Fermi energy.

\[f_F(E)\approx \exp\left[-\frac{E-E_F}{k_BT}\right]\]
\[E-E_F\approx 3k_BT\quad \Rightarrow\quad \text{about 5% error}\]

Error plot

Degenerate semiconductor

At very high doping, \(E_F\) can enter the conduction band for degenerate n-type material or the valence band for degenerate p-type material. Then the simple Boltzmann approximation is no longer accurate.

\[N_d \text{ very large}\Rightarrow E_F\ge E_c\]
\[N_a \text{ very large}\Rightarrow E_F\le E_v\]

Boltzmann validity example
