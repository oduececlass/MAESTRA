Math + physical meaning

Step 1 of 6
Thermal population above the barrier
\[n_{m0}=N_C\exp\!\left(-\frac{q\phi_{Bn}}{kT}\right)\]

Reverse bias

⇄

The semiconductor-to-metal flux is suppressed. The metal-to-semiconductor flux remains nearly unchanged, so \(J \to -J_s\).

Thermal equilibrium

⇄

Equal opposing thermionic fluxes produce zero net current even though electrons continue crossing in both directions: \(J_{s\to m}=J_{m\to s}\), so \(J=0\).

Forward bias

⇄

The band bending falls from \(V_{bi}\) to \(V_{bi}-V\), so the activation energy falls from \(\phi_{Bn}\) to \(\phi_{Bn}-V\) and that flux grows by \(\exp(V/V_T)\).

Model boundaries. The ideal Richardson thermionic-emission law assumes carriers pass over the barrier. Real Schottky contacts can also show image-force barrier lowering, interface states, tunneling/thermionic-field emission, an ideality factor different from 1, series resistance, and high-injection effects. The depletion width shown uses the depletion approximation and is a teaching aid; it is not part of the Richardson current prefactor.
