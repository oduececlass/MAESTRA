Schottky + Ohmic
7.1.1 Physical picture: work function, electron affinity, and barrier height

Professional band-diagram reconstruction

This section recreates the ideal metal–semiconductor energy-band picture from the chapter using cleaner notation, larger labels, and a tutorial-friendly layout. Panel (a) shows the isolated materials before contact. Panel (b) shows the metal–semiconductor contact at thermal equilibrium, where the Fermi level is flat and the semiconductor bands bend to satisfy electrostatic equilibrium.

Before contact

The metal work function \(\phi_m\) and the semiconductor work function \(\phi_s\) are generally different. The electron affinity \(\chi\) measures the energy gap from the vacuum level down to the conduction-band edge \(E_C\).

\[q\phi_{Bn}=q\phi_m-q\chi\quad\Rightarrow\quad \phi_{Bn}=\phi_m-\chi\]

After contact

Once contact is made, the Fermi levels align at equilibrium. The conduction and valence bands bend in the semiconductor, creating the built-in potential \(V_{bi}\) and the depletion region under the Schottky contact.

\[V_{bi}=\phi_{Bn}-V_n,\qquad V_n=V_T\ln\!\left(\frac{N_C}{N_D}\right)\]

This figure shows the aligned Fermi level after contact, the band bending in the semiconductor, and the key energetic quantities \(q\phi_{Bn}\), \(qV_{bi}\), \(qV_n\), and \(q\phi_s\).

Equation (1) · Electron barrier height
\[q\phi_{Bn}=q\phi_m-q\chi\]
Equation (2) · Hole barrier height
\[q\phi_{Bp}=E_g-\left(q\phi_m-q\chi\right)\]
Equation (3) · Barrier-height sum rule
\[q\left(\phi_{Bn}+\phi_{Bp}\right)=E_g\]

Notation. \(\phi_m\) is the metal work function, \(\chi\) is the semiconductor electron affinity, \(\phi_s\) is the semiconductor work function, \(\phi_{Bn}\) and \(\phi_{Bp}\) are the electron and hole barrier heights, \(V_n\) is the energy spacing between \(E_C\) and \(E_F\) in the neutral n-type region, and \(V_{bi}\) is the built-in potential on the semiconductor side.

These diagrams are especially useful for understanding why the MESFET gate behaves as a rectifying Schottky contact.

Energy-band diagrams for n-type and p-type metal–semiconductor contacts. The panels compare equilibrium, forward bias, and reverse bias using the quantities \(q\phi_{Bn}\), \(q\phi_{Bp}\), \(qV_{bi}\), \(qV_F\), and \(qV_R\).
n-type Schottky contact
\[q\phi_{Bn} = q(\phi_m-\chi), \qquad V_{bi} = \phi_{Bn} - V_n\]
p-type Schottky contact
\[q\phi_{Bp} = E_g - q\phi_{Bn}, \qquad V_{bi} = \phi_p - \phi_m\]
Forward bias
\[\text{effective\ barrier} = q(V_{bi}-V_F)\]
Reverse bias
\[\text{effective\ barrier} = q(V_{bi}+V_R)\]
MESFET connection. In a MESFET, the gate is a Schottky contact on an n-type channel. A more positive reverse gate bias increases depletion and narrows the channel, while forward bias reduces the barrier and is usually avoided in normal operation.
7.1.3 Advanced math: depletion width, electric field, and capacitance

Using the depletion approximation for a uniformly doped n-type semiconductor, the charge density is approximately \(\rho=qN_D\) inside the depletion region. Poisson's equation then gives a linearly varying electric field.

\[ |\mathcal{E}(x)|=\frac{qN_D}{\varepsilon_s}(W-x),\qquad \mathcal{E}_{m}=\frac{qN_DW}{\varepsilon_s} \] \[ V_{bi}-V=\frac{qN_DW^2}{2\varepsilon_s},\qquad W=\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_s(V_{bi}-V)}{qN_D}} \] \[ C=\frac{\varepsilon_s}{W},\qquad \frac{1}{C^2}=\frac{2(V_{bi}-V)}{q\varepsilon_sN_D} \]
Step 1. Start from \(d\mathcal{E}/dx=\rho/\varepsilon_s=qN_D/\varepsilon_s\).
Step 2. Use \(\mathcal{E}(W)=0\) to obtain \(\mathcal{E}(x)=-(qN_D/\varepsilon_s)(W-x)\).
Step 3. Integrate the field area: \(V_{bi}-V=\int_0^W |\mathcal{E}(x)|dx=qN_DW^2/(2\varepsilon_s)\).
7.1.3 Ohmic contacts: why high doping reduces contact resistance

An ohmic contact should pass current with a small voltage drop compared with the active device region. Low-resistance ohmic behavior can be obtained by lowering the barrier height, using very high doping, or both. High doping narrows the depletion width and increases tunneling probability.

\[ R_c=\left(\frac{\partial J}{\partial V}\right)^{-1}_{V=0} \] \[ R_c\propto \exp\!\left(\frac{q\phi_{Bn}}{kT}\right)\quad\text{for thermionic emission} \] \[ R_c\sim \exp\!\left(\frac{C_2\phi_{Bn}}{\sqrt{N_D}}\right)\quad\text{for tunneling-dominated transport} \]
This reference plot shows how the specific contact resistance \(R_c\) changes with doping and Schottky barrier height. Lower barrier heights and heavier doping strongly reduce \(R_c\), which is why ohmic source and drain contacts are often formed using heavily doped semiconductor regions.
Design insight. The interactive ohmic-contact simulator below follows the same core trend seen in this plot: increasing \(N_D\) or reducing \(\phi_B\) decreases the contact resistance dramatically.

7.2 MESFET

Device structure, depletion control, pinch-off, saturation, and the drain-current model.

Gate-Controlled Channel
7.2.1 Device structure and channel resistance

Geometry

The MESFET has a Schottky gate and ohmic source/drain contacts on a doped channel above a semi-insulating substrate. Key dimensions are gate length \(L\), width \(Z\), channel thickness \(a\), and depletion width \(W\).

Channel resistance

The conducting cross-section is reduced from \(Za\) to \(Z(a-W)\). As \(W\) increases, channel resistance rises.

\[R=\frac{L}{q\mu_n N_D Z(a-W)}\]

Figure · MESFET structure and equivalent cross-sectional view

Panel (a) shows a three-dimensional device view. The source and drain are ohmic contacts so carriers can enter and leave the channel with low resistance. The metal gate forms a Schottky contact to the semiconductor, so the gate voltage controls depletion under the gate. The passivation / insulator helps protect the surface and defines the active gate region. Panel (b) shows the corresponding cross-sectional view with terminals \(V_S\), \(V_G\), and the grounded drain, highlighting the channel directly under the gate.
How MESFET control works. When the gate is reverse biased relative to the n-type channel, the depletion region beneath the Schottky gate widens into the semiconductor. That reduces the effective channel thickness and increases the channel resistance. If the depletion region becomes deep enough, the channel pinches off and the drain current saturates.
7.2.2 Pinch-off and saturation

As drain voltage increases, the channel potential rises from source to drain. The gate-to-channel reverse bias is largest near the drain, so depletion is deepest there. Pinch-off occurs when the depletion region reaches the channel thickness.

\[ V_P=\frac{qN_Da^2}{2\varepsilon_s} \] \[ V_{Dsat}=V_P-V_G-V_{bi} \] \[ W(y)=\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_s[V(y)+V_G+V_{bi}]}{qN_D}} \]
Important sign convention: for an n-channel depletion-mode MESFET, \(V_G\le 0\). A more negative gate voltage increases depletion width and lowers the saturation current.
7.2.3 Current-voltage characteristic

Before pinch-off, the current follows from integrating the differential channel resistance along the gate length.

\[ I_D=I_P\left[\frac{V_D}{V_P}-\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{V_D+V_G+V_{bi}}{V_P}\right)^{3/2}+\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{V_G+V_{bi}}{V_P}\right)^{3/2}\right] \] \[ I_P=\frac{Z\mu_nq^2N_D^2a^3}{2\varepsilon_sL} \] \[ I_{Dsat}=I_P\left[\frac{1}{3}-\left(\frac{V_G+V_{bi}}{V_P}\right)+\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{V_G+V_{bi}}{V_P}\right)^{3/2}\right] \]

7.2.4 High-frequency performance

The cutoff frequency \(f_T\) is the frequency at which the small-signal output current no longer exceeds the input charging current. The result emphasizes short gate length and high carrier velocity.

\[ \tilde{i}_{in}=2\pi fC_G\tilde{v}_g,\qquad \tilde{i}_{out}=g_m\tilde{v}_g \] \[ f_T=\frac{g_m}{2\pi C_G}\approx \frac{\mu_n qN_Da^2}{2\pi\varepsilon_sL^2} \] \[ \text{velocity-saturation limit:}\qquad f_T\approx \frac{v_s}{2\pi L} \]

7.3 MODFET / HEMT

Modulation doping, heterojunction confinement, 2DEG formation, and mobility enhancement.

High-Mobility Channel
7.3.1 Why modulation doping increases mobility

MESFET limitation

The channel is doped, so electrons move through a region containing ionized donors. Ionized impurity scattering reduces mobility, especially at low temperature.

MODFET advantage

Dopants are placed in a wider-bandgap layer. Electrons transfer to the narrow-bandgap undoped channel, creating a 2DEG separated from the donors by a spacer.

\[ n_s\approx \eta N_D d_1,\qquad R_{sheet}\approx \frac{1}{q\mu n_s} \] \[ I_D\propto \mu n_s\left(V_G-V_T\right)^2 \]

Practice and guided checks

Use these questions after exploring the simulations.

Self-test
1. Why does reverse bias increase Schottky depletion width?

Relate your answer to \(V_{bi}-V\).

Reverse bias makes \(V\) negative, so \(V_{bi}-V\) becomes larger. Because \(W\propto\sqrt{V_{bi}-V}\), the depletion region widens.
2. What happens to MESFET \(V_{Dsat}\) as \(V_G\) becomes more negative?

Use \(V_{Dsat}=V_P-V_G-V_{bi}\).

With the sign convention in this tutor, more negative \(V_G\) increases the drain voltage needed to reach the same depletion condition, but it also reduces available channel opening and saturation current.
3. Why is an ohmic contact usually heavily doped?

Think about barrier width and tunneling.

Heavy doping reduces depletion width. A narrower barrier greatly increases tunneling probability, reducing specific contact resistance.
4. Why can MODFET mobility exceed MESFET mobility?

Use the idea of modulation doping.

The electrons conduct in an undoped channel separated from ionized donors, so impurity scattering is reduced and mobility can become much higher.
