The Frozen Sluice

A canal carries water from the upstream pool to the downstream outlet. A chilled plate hangs over it. The colder the plate, the thicker the ice grows down into the flow — and because the water surface sits lower downstream, the ice always grows into a wedge. When the wedge touches the bed, the canal is pinched, and pushing harder downstream stops buying you more flow.

Model assumptions and equations

First-order Shockley gradual-channel MESFET, abrupt depletion approximation, constant mobility — no velocity saturation, so currents run optimistic for short gates.

$$ \begin{aligned} V_P &= \frac{qN_D a^2}{2\varepsilon}, \\ V_T &= V_{bi}-V_P, \qquad V_{bi}=0.80\,\mathrm{V}, \\ I_P &= g_0V_P, \qquad g_0=\frac{qN_D\mu aZ}{L}, \\ W(x) &= a\sqrt{\frac{V_{bi}-V_{GS}+V(x)}{V_P}}, \\ I_D &= I_P\Bigg[ \frac{V}{V_P} -\frac{2}{3}\Bigg( \left(\frac{V+V_{bi}-V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)^{3/2} \\ &\hspace{8.0em} -\left(\frac{V_{bi}-V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)^{3/2} \Bigg)\Bigg]. \end{aligned} $$

The ice profile is not a sketch: for every column of pixels the channel potential $V(x)$ is solved by bisection from the constant-current condition, then $W(x)$ follows from it. That is why the wedge steepens toward the drain as $V_{DS}$ rises.

Depletion-mode device: the canal conducts at VGS = 0 and must be chilled shut. Forward gate bias is limited to +0.4 V — beyond that the Schottky plate starts conducting and the metaphor (and the device) breaks down.
