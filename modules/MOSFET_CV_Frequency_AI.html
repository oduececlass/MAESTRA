Physical interpretation — the small-signal picture

A C–V meter adds a tiny AC wiggle \(\delta v\) on top of the DC gate voltage and measures the responding charge \(\delta Q\): \(C=\delta Q/\delta v\). The responding charge acts as the hidden second plate. The closer that plate is to the gate, the larger the measured capacitance.

Detailed symbolic and numerical explanation

Current operating-point explanation

Current case selected by \(V_{\mathrm{GS}}\) and frequency

1. Accumulation

Majority carriers gather at the oxide–silicon interface, so the semiconductor behaves almost like a conducting plate.

\[ C_{\mathrm{acc}} \approx C_{\mathrm{ox}} \]

2. Depletion

The oxide capacitance and depletion capacitance are in series.

\[ C_{\mathrm{dep}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{si}}}{W_d} \]
\[ \frac{1}{C}=\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{ox}}}+\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{dep}}} \]
\[ C=\frac{C_{\mathrm{ox}}C_{\mathrm{dep}}}{C_{\mathrm{ox}}+C_{\mathrm{dep}}} \]

3. Low-frequency inversion

Minority carriers follow the slow AC signal and provide incremental inversion charge.

\[ C_{\mathrm{inv,LF}} \approx C_{\mathrm{ox}} \]

4. High-frequency inversion

Minority carriers cannot follow the fast AC signal, so the maximum depletion width remains in series with the oxide.

\[ C_{\mathrm{min}}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{ox}}C_{\mathrm{dep,max}}}{C_{\mathrm{ox}}+C_{\mathrm{dep,max}}} \]
\[ C_{\mathrm{dep,max}}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{ox}}C_{\mathrm{min}}}{C_{\mathrm{ox}}-C_{\mathrm{min}}} \]
The numerical model is illustrative and normalized for teaching. A physical device model would calculate \(W_d\), \(C_{\mathrm{dep}}\), and \(V_{\mathrm{T}}\) from oxide thickness, substrate doping, temperature, and semiconductor permittivity.
