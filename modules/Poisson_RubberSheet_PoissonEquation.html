Guided laboratory

Work one activity at a time: predict, test, explain, and connect to the equation.

Project 1

Source charge and curvature

  1. Load Opposite charge sheets.
  2. Compare the source \(\rho(x,y)\) with the curvature \(\nabla^2\phi\) on the centerline.
  3. Explain why the sheet bends where the source charge lives.
  4. State the local rule: large \(|\rho|\) creates large curvature magnitude.
Project 2

Sign of \(\rho\) and sign of curvature

Compare Single positive source and Single negative source. Connect the source sign to whether the sheet bends up or down.

Key idea: \(\nabla^2\phi=-\rho/\epsilon\) fixes the sign relation.

Project 3

Slope, gradient, and field direction

Turn on the arrows: they show \(-\nabla\phi\). Distinguish height \(\phi\), slope \(\nabla\phi\), and curvature \(\nabla^2\phi\).

Project 4

Superposition of multiple sources

Load a dipole, a same-sign pair, and a quadrupole. The total surface is the combined contribution of all sources.

Key idea: Poisson is linear, so \(\rho=\rho_1+\rho_2+\cdots\) means \(\phi\) superposes too.

Project 5

Permittivity controls curvature strength

Move ε. For the same source charge, larger ε reduces the curvature magnitude: \(\nabla^2\phi=-\rho/\epsilon\).

Project 6

Smoothing, localization, and numerical intuition

Move σ. Small σ makes a localized source; larger σ spreads it out. See why localized sources produce sharp curvature and why simulations often smooth them.

Project 7

From rubber-sheet observation to Poisson's equation

  1. Observe where the sheet bends most strongly.
  2. Compare that with the source map \(\rho(x,y)\).
  3. Use the curvature trace to identify \(\nabla^2\phi\).
  4. Use arrows / centerline slope to identify \(-\nabla\phi\).
  5. Write \(\nabla^2\phi=-\rho/\epsilon\).
Project 8

Bridge: from placed charge to a real junction

Load Opposite charge sheets and shrink σ so the two sheets become sharp. You have just hand-built the depletion dipole of a PN junction.

Then switch models. In the PN Diode tab that same dipole appears on its own, with its magnitude set by doping and its width set by the bias — you no longer choose it, the physics does.

Project 1

Built-in potential at equilibrium

  1. Load Equilibrium junction (\(V_a=0\)).
  2. Observe the step even though no battery is connected.
  3. Read \(V_{bi}\) and compare with \(V_{bi}=V_T\ln(N_AN_D/n_i^2)\).
  4. Explain which charges create the step.
Project 2

Depletion dipole and curvature

On the centerline, \(\rho(x)\) is an abrupt box: \(-qN_A\) for \(-x_p<x<0\) and \(+qN_D\) for \(0<x<x_n\).

Key idea: constant \(\rho\) means constant curvature, so each side of the sheet is a parabola.

Project 3

Reverse bias: wider depletion, taller step

  1. Slide \(V_a\) from 0 toward −10 and watch W, xp, xn.
  2. Predict before you look: does |E|max grow or shrink?
  3. Verify \(W\propto\sqrt{V_{bi}-V_a}\) by comparing two bias values.
Project 4

Forward bias: narrower depletion, lower step

Slide \(V_a\) positive. The step \(V_{bi}-V_a\) shrinks and the depletion region narrows — which is why real diode current grows exponentially.

Note: \(V_a\) is clamped just below \(V_{bi}\); at \(V_a=V_{bi}\) the depletion approximation breaks down.

Project 5

Asymmetric doping and one-sided junctions

Set \(N_A \gg N_D\) (p⁺n). The depletion region lives almost entirely on the lightly doped side, because charge balance requires \(N_Ax_p=N_Dx_n\).

Project 6

Peak electric field and breakdown intuition

  1. Turn on the field arrows. Where is the field largest?
  2. Increase reverse bias and watch |E|max.
  3. Real junctions break down when |E|max reaches a critical value.
Project 7

Electron-energy view: who rolls downhill?

Toggle the electron-energy view. The sheet flips to \(-q\phi\): the n-side becomes the valley, the p-side the hill.

Key idea: electrons minimize energy, not potential. Holes see the original sheet as their barrier; electrons see the flipped one.

Project 8

Misconceptions and exit reflection

Explain what the rubber sheet represents in a diode and what it does not — it shows electrostatics, not carrier flow or recombination.
